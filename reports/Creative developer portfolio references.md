# Build a resume that rings like a call

The strongest recipe for Leon Kazaryan's site is a single concept drawn from his actual work, with a conventional, fast content skeleton underneath it. In the verified evidence, awards go to a metaphor tied to the owner's identity (Bruno Simon's drivable 3D car, Mat Voyce's "the type is the interface"), not to a bag of effects. The recommended concept is a **telephony-plus-CRM hybrid**: the site opens as an incoming call (a nod to Twilio, JustCall and Zoiper), scrolls as a deal moving down a pipeline (AmoCRM, Pipedrive, HubSpot), logs his three wins as "Closed Won" entries, and ends at a dial-pad contact. The mechanics can be copied from verified sources: GSAP SplitText kinetic type, Lenis smooth scroll synced to GSAP ScrollTrigger, shader uniforms tweened by GSAP, a boot-style loader that hides real asset and shader work, a terminal easter egg hidden from `help`, and a pointer-gated custom cursor. The recommended stack is Next.js App Router, GSAP with `useGSAP`, `lenis/react` and one lazy-loaded React Three Fiber canvas. Two cautions shape everything below. First, no source verified an award-winning portfolio with a telephony, hackathon or counter-wall concept, so that niche is open but also untested. Second, nearly all implementation detail on top-tier sites comes from secondary write-ups, because Codrops and Awwwards pages often returned 403 to the researchers. The "Inference" labels below mark where the researchers or I are reasoning rather than quoting.

## What the verified award winners actually prove

The clearest evidence is Bruno Simon's portfolio. The 2019 version was a Three.js world where the visitor drives a toy car, and it won Awwwards Site of the Month for November, with five sections: intro, a crossroads of statues and signposts, projects, personal info and contact, and a physics playground ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/bruno-simon-portfolio-wins-site-of-the-month-november.html)). Collisions ran on simplified Cannon.js primitives synced to the Three.js scene each frame, and the site also took the 2019 Developer Site of the Year User's Choice ([Awwwards annual awards 2019](https://awwwards.com/annual-awards-2019); [Bruno's case study](https://medium.com/@bruno_simon/bruno-simon-portfolio-case-study-960402cc259b?rel=muzli)). A newer version took Awwwards Site of the Day on Jan 21, 2026 (score 8.11) plus a Developer Award (7.65) ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/brunos-portfolio)). Secondary sources report 400,000+ visitors, and they disagree on whether the 2019 site won Site of the Year: Creative Bloq says yes, Pastel says nominated ([Pastel](https://usepastel.com/blog/how-a-design-portfolio-got-the-attention-of-400-000-visitors); [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/news/3d-car-portfolio)). Critics call it slow and hard to navigate as a normal homepage, which is the lesson for Leon: any concept needs a plain, fast route to the content.

The second verified concept-led site is Mat Voyce's kinetic-typography portfolio, built by developer Hon Tran with Uncommon studio and designer Huy Phan. It is reported as Awwwards Site of the Day in January 2025 and a nominee for GSAP Site of the Year 2025, on Next.js, GSAP (SplitText, ScrollTrigger), a Strapi CMS and selective WebGL ([Hon Tran case study](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/mat-voyce-case-study-award-winning-portfolio)). Its structure (intro, credits, brief, craft, results) shows the pattern worth copying: the metaphor shapes transitions while a conventional skeleton carries the content. The case study is a developer's own account, and the matvoyce.tv domain comes from a search snippet.

Other developer-built references are thinner. Minh Pham's portfolio (case study by Hon Tran) is reported as Awwwards Site of the Day with a developer score of 7.77 and a SOTD score of 7.37, using GSAP, ScrollTrigger, inertial scrolling and `gsap.matchMedia()` for reduced motion ([Hon Tran](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/minh-pham-portfolio-case-study)). Stefan Vitasović's Portfolio 2025 solved the problem of merging a WebGL scroll with navigation scroll through a shared scroll engine, but that comes from a search summary only because the Codrops page returned 403 ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/03/05/case-study-stefan-vitasovic-portfolio-2025/)). Studio and brand sites show the scroll-to-camera pattern, per one secondary roundup: Lusion's Oryzo (Awwwards Site of the Month, April 2026, scroll moves the camera on Z), Explore Primland (SOTD, Feb 2026, terrain flythrough) and Cartier Watches & Wonders 2026 (six 3D "rooms" using Three.js, GLSL, GSAP, Lenis and Web Audio) ([Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/best-threejs-websites-2026)). Those awards rest on a single roundup and were not checked on Awwwards itself, and none is a personal portfolio.

The researchers found no verified award-winning portfolio built on a phone, chat or telephony metaphor, none on a hackathon trophy or podium metaphor, and no named award-winning stat or achievement counter wall. Only lower-authority DEV.to examples exist (chat-based, split GUI/terminal, retro Windows 95, cyberpunk terminal), plus a 2017 vintage-OS chat console on One Page Love ([DEV](https://dev.to/nasimkuniyil/breaking-the-traditional-portfolio-my-chat-based-approach-4298); [One Page Love](https://onepagelove.com/heather-vandervecht)). DEV's portfolio-challenge commentary adds that winners made it obvious within three seconds what they do and have built ([DEV](https://dev.to/devteam/congrats-to-the-new-year-new-you-portfolio-challenge-winners-and-runner-ups-1l9h/comments)). That is evidence for a clear hero, and the absence of telephony examples is an opportunity for a distinctive concept, not proof it will win.

## Techniques worth copying, and what is verified about each

**Scroll animation.** The official Lenis README gives the sync recipe: `lenis.on('scroll', ScrollTrigger.update)`, `gsap.ticker.add(t => lenis.raf(t*1000))` and `gsap.ticker.lagSmoothing(0)` ([Lenis README](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis/blob/main/README.md)). The React binding uses `ReactLenis` from `lenis/react` with `autoRaf: false` and a ticker callback removed on cleanup ([Lenis React README](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis/blob/main/packages/react/README.md)). Both Pham and Voyce share one easing and duration token set across all reveals, with Voyce using `back.out` for entrances and a softer `expo.out` for ScrollTrigger section reveals ([Hon Tran](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/mat-voyce-case-study-award-winning-portfolio)). No verified portfolio was documented using pinned horizontal timelines or stacked cards; that is standard GSAP (`pin`, `scrub`, an `x` tween), but it is general knowledge here, not a cited example.

**Kinetic typography.** Voyce's documented technique is splitting text into characters and words, staggering transforms and opacity, `back.out` overshoot, random per-character rotation, animating only transform and opacity, splitting once and reusing the timeline, and honoring `prefers-reduced-motion` ([Hon Tran](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/mat-voyce-case-study-award-winning-portfolio)). GSAP and all its plugins, SplitText included, are now free, and SplitText's `aria: "auto"` option labels the split element and hides the fragments from screen readers ([Webflow](https://webflow.com/blog/gsap-becomes-free); [GSAP React docs](https://gsap.com/resources/React/) for the React hook). Awwwards inspiration pages exist for kinetic type heroes but carry little detail ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/kinetic-type-with-twist-distortion)).

**Shader and WebGL backgrounds.** Pham's site blends outgoing and incoming textures in a fragment shader that displaces UVs with a noise map, with GSAP driving the blend uniform. The scene initializes lazily, the render loop pauses off-screen and pixel ratio is capped at 2 ([Hon Tran](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/minh-pham-portfolio-case-study)). Arnaud Rocca's portfolio (Nuxt/Vue, Codrops March 2026) pairs a reusable GSAP effect system with optional fluid WebGL, though the fluid implementation was not in the retrieved text ([Engineered.at](https://engineered.at/articles/arnaud-rocca-s-portfolio-from-a-gsap-powered-motion-system-to-fluid-webgl)). Hubtown (Awwwards SOTD, June 2026) uses a mouse-reveal that uncovers geometry and lighting in a Three.js scene, per the Utsubo roundup. The portable idea is that a shader uniform is just another GSAP tween target, so DOM and WebGL share one timeline (inference, built on Pham's verified description).

**Cinematic loaders.** Evidence here is thin. Joffrey Spitzer's Astro/GSAP/Lenis/Three.js portfolio has a preloader that mainly loads media and key assets ([Codrops, Feb 2026](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/02/18/joffrey-spitzer-portfolio-a-minimalist-astro-gsap-build-with-reveals-flip-transitions-and-subtle-motion/)). Gabriel Contassot's 2024 portfolio uses a purely theatrical block of changing numbers that does not track real loading ([Codrops, Apr 2024](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2024/04/24/case-study-gabriel-contassots-portfolio-2024/)). A 2026 Codrops article on a 3D portfolio uses the preloader phase to precompile shaders with `gl.compileAsync()` ([Codrops, Jun 2026](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/06/11/sketching-the-impossible-a-3d-portfolio-built-without-a-single-3d-model/)). All three are search-excerpt level because the pages returned 403. Terminal boot sequences double as loaders: Vlad Burca's site lists a "Terminal Boot Sequence with System Init Animation" in Awwwards' Loading category ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/terminal-boot-sequence-with-system-init-animation-vlad-burca-product-designer)). No source gave data on ideal duration, skip rates or bounce impact.

**Terminal easter eggs.** Vlad Burca's CLI-style portfolio (vladburca.com) is the only terminal site with Awwwards recognition in the notes, an Honorable Mention on 26 March 2026, built in plain HTML/CSS/JS with autocomplete and a glitchy CLI-style 404. Its community usability votes ranged from 4 to 9, so it polarizes ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/vlad-burca-product-designer)). No terminal portfolio was verified as a Site of the Day or FWA winner. Among open-source examples, Omganesh014's React 19/xterm.js build offers a "Recruiter Mode" role-based tour with a 3-minute inspection path ([GitHub](https://github.com/Omganesh014/Terminal-Based-Portfolio)). One PR documents the best easter-egg practice: jokes like `sudo`, `rm -rf /`, `fortune` and `hire-me` are excluded from `help` and Tab completion, `help` ends with a teaser that not every command is listed, and `prefers-reduced-motion` removes fade-ins ([USER1043 PR #7](https://github.com/USER1043/PortfolioWebsite/pull/7)). Bruno Salgado's site documents six eggs tied to the number six (Konami code, six taps on a badge, `?trace=6` in the URL) with Esc to dismiss and reduced-motion respected, but no keyboard path for the tap eggs ([Bruno Salgado](https://brunosalgado.dev/writing/six-small-easter-eggs-hidden-in-this-portfolio)). Termfolio maps URLs to terminal state (`/projects` runs `cd projects && ls`), though its page returned 404 and the claim rests on a search summary ([search result](https://solomonneas.dev/projects/termfolio)).

**Custom cursors.** The verified evidence is about libraries, not live winners. Blobity is a canvas blob cursor with spring physics and `data-blobity-tooltip` labels, GPLv3 with a commercial license and essentially no touch support ([docsearch listing](https://docsearch.algolia.com/mcp/docs/repo/gmrchk/blobity)). Intent Label on 21st.dev is MIT and morphs a dot into a labeled pill via `data-intent`, with keyboard-focus support ([21st.dev](https://21st.dev/@ham7a311/components/intent-label.md)). Custom cursors never reach touch users ([Impact Media](https://www.impactmedia.co.uk/insights/add-some-fun-and-practicality-with-custom-cursors/)).

**Achievement counters.** Only techniques were found, no named winning wall. Options include tweening a plain object with GSAP and writing text in `onUpdate`, CountUp.js at about 5kb, and odometer-style digit-roll components ([learnwithhasan](https://learnwithhasan.com/js-libraries/countup-js/); [21st.dev Odometer Stats](https://21st.dev/@ham7a311/components/odometer-stats.md)). With only three wins, a classic count-up wall is weak (inference); the concept below turns wins into log entries and uses counters only for real numbers.

## Recommended stack and how to wire it safely

Use **Next.js App Router with React 19, GSAP plus `@gsap/react`, `lenis/react`, and React Three Fiber v9 for a single canvas**. R3F v9 pairs with React 19, and Next.js may need `transpilePackages: ['three']` ([R3F installation docs](https://github.com/pmndrs/react-three-fiber/blob/master/docs/getting-started/installation.mdx)). `useGSAP` replaces `useEffect` with `gsap.context()` cleanup, takes a `scope` ref, handles React Strict Mode double effects, and requires `"use client"` in the App Router ([GSAP React docs](https://gsap.com/resources/React/)). Per Next.js docs (updated 2026-03-10), `next/dynamic` with `ssr: false` works only inside Client Components ([Next.js lazy loading](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/lazy-loading)). R3F's `<Canvas frameloop="demand">` renders only on change ([R3F scaling performance](https://github.com/pmndrs/react-three-fiber/blob/master/docs/advanced/scaling-performance.mdx)). Mobile GPUs struggle with complex scenes and heavy postprocessing, so 3D should be gated by capability and reduced-motion checks with a static fallback ([fixdevs](https://fixdevs.com/blog/react-three-fiber-not-working/)). Stack choice is also corroborated by Cartier's reported use of GSAP, Lenis and Three.js (Utsubo, above), and by Mat Voyce's Next.js base.

The following wiring is inference, not sourced from docs: mount one `SmoothScroll` client provider in the root layout; register ScrollTrigger once and refresh it after fonts load; wrap animations in `gsap.matchMedia()` under `(prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)` and skip Lenis otherwise; keep the LCP element as server-rendered HTML text so the canvas never gates it; dynamically import a single small full-screen fragment-shader canvas (`dpr={[1,1.5]}`) after idle, pause it off-screen, and fall back to a CSS gradient on mobile and reduced motion; use `tabular-nums` for counters; gate the cursor behind `(hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`. The researchers did not verify current package versions (check with `npm view` before pinning), R3F v9 on Next 16 specifically, Lenis behavior across route changes, or Core Web Vitals thresholds from an official page. They also found no source measuring the cost of canvas cursors, and no source on loader duration. Treat the build order as a judgment call: Lenis and ScrollTrigger sync first, then scrubbed DOM text reveals with one token set, then one shader plane driven by a GSAP uniform, and a scroll-linked camera path last and only if time allows. Skip a Bruno-style physics world, which is a large solo effort and a poor navigation model.

## Concept: "Incoming call" into a pipeline of wins

All ideas in this section are proposals, not existing sites. The framing is that Leon's resume items map directly onto the verified pattern "metaphor equals the owner's work." The researchers' top pick, which I endorse, combines two of their five ideas, because it is distinctive, explains his profession and needs only DOM, SVG and GSAP rather than heavy WebGL.

The **loader** is a softphone boot: a log such as "registering SIP... loading fonts... compiling shader" whose lines reflect real tasks (assets, fonts, `gl.compileAsync()` per the Codrops 2026 pattern). It runs once per session, has a skip key, and is short (inferences; no source supplies timing data). The **hero** is a ringing incoming call with an animated waveform behind kinetic SplitText of "Leon Kazaryan, full-stack / React Native developer," with an Accept button that starts the story. The background is the one shader canvas, a soft noise field whose intensity follows the waveform and cursor-position uniform (the Hubtown and Pham patterns). The **scroll journey** is a call timeline with a live call timer: Salesautomators comes first as the "line" he works on (Front-End and Mobile Dev, Twilio, JustCall, Zoiper), with a routing graph of nodes for telephony providers connected to CRM nodes (AmoCRM, Pipedrive, HubSpot), and scroll routes a call through the graph in cheap SVG. A pipeline kanban follows, Lead, Qualified, Demo, Won, where a deal card travels down as the user scrolls, using the shared GSAP timeline and Lenis sync described above.

The **wins** become call-log and "Closed Won" entries rather than a count-up wall: "HackNU 2026, Won, 1st place," "Business Cup 2026, Won, 1st place," "nFactorial Incubator, Won, 3rd place," optionally presented as a podium or arcade leaderboard. Counters appear only for real, verifiable numbers he supplies (integrations shipped, events entered); inventing figures would undercut the credibility the whole site depends on. The **terminal easter egg** is a hidden dev console opened by the Konami code or a key sequence, with commands hidden from `help` but hinted at, per the USER1043 pattern: `whoami` as the story entry (as in Terminal OS), `hire-me`, `sudo`, plus a `dial` command that opens the contact pad. Add an Esc exit and a reduced-motion variant, and give every tap-based trigger a keyboard path, the gap in Bruno Salgado's eggs. The **custom cursor** is a small dot that morphs into a labeled pill ("Answer," "Play," "Open case") via `data-intent`-style attributes, mouse-only, with the native cursor kept for touch and keyboard users. The **contact** section is a dial pad whose CTA places a mailto or call link.

Usability safeguards come from the verified weaknesses of existing sites. Add a visible "classic view" toggle and a no-typing recruiter path (clickable command chips and a 3-minute tour modeled on Omganesh014's Recruiter Mode), because Burca's 4-to-9 usability spread and Simon's navigation critique show that concept-heavy sites cost usability points. Keep a parallel semantic HTML version of the content: xterm.js-style terminals have weak screen-reader support, which is the researchers' own reasoning with no source found. Open the page with a statement of what he does and what he has won, so the three-second test from DEV's challenge commentary passes. If a plainer fallback is wanted, the "compiler/terminal" metaphor (build output as a boot log) is the cheapest alternative, but it is the most common in the DEV.to examples and the least distinctive.

## Conclusion

The research changes the shopping list: most of what feels like "award-level" craft is a small set of repeatable mechanics (one scroll clock, one easing token set, uniforms tweened on the same timeline, lazy and pausable WebGL) that a solo developer can copy from documented sources, while the metaphor is the part that cannot be copied and must come from Leon's own work. Telephony and CRM integration is both his real day job and an unclaimed niche in everything the researchers found, which is why the incoming-call-to-pipeline concept beats the more common terminal or OS ideas.

The main uncertainty is evidentiary. Several winners' awards rest on one roundup, many technical details are search-snippet level, no terminal portfolio is a verified Site of the Day, and nothing verified shows how a telephony or achievement-wall concept would be scored. Before building, verify the Awwwards and FWA pages for the cited sites, pin library versions, and test the shipped site for Lighthouse and reduced-motion behavior, since none of the sources measured cost or recruiter response.
